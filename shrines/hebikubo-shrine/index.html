Hebikubo Shrine

蛇窪神社

The White Snake and Dragon of Wealth

Home › Guides › Hebikubo Shrine

Originally known as Kamishinmei Tenso Shrine before its official renaming on May 1, 2019 (Reiwa 1), Hebikubo Shrine stands as one of Tokyo's most unique spiritual destinations. Famous for its 8-meter white dragon statue and profound connections to serpentine folklore, it has evolved from an ancient drought-relief sanctuary into a modern epicenter for wealth, business prosperity, and spiritual purification.

1322 Drought & Historical Origins

In 1322 (Genko 2), a severe drought devastated Musashi Province. Homitsu Shonin, the 2nd head priest of Gonsho-ji, performed a 7-day fasting rain prayer (amagoi no danjiki kigan) at the Ryujin shrine northwest of his temple. Grateful local farmers officially founded the shrine the following year, in 1323 (Genko 3).

The striking 8-meter concrete white dragon that watches over the grounds today was constructed and donated much later, on February 5, 1975, by local scaffolding contractor Katsushi Manabe.

Serpentine Folklore & Benzaiten

Urban development destroyed the shrine's original spring-fed washing area, forcing the resident white snake to migrate to a pond in what is now Togoshi Park (formerly the Edo-period Hosokawa daimyo estate). According to local lore, the snake appeared in a dream to community leader Moriya Tomokichi asking to return home. Moriya and the chief priest built a new pond and island, inviting the divided spirit of Benzaiten from Itsukushima Shrine in Miyajima to enshrine the snake properly.

The Rare Yumemifuda Amulet

The highly coveted Yumemifuda amulet contains genuine shed skin of the Iwakuni White Snake (Elaphe climacophora), a species designated as a National Natural Monument (Kennen Kinenbutsu) by the Japanese government. It costs 2,000 JPY, is sold exclusively during New Year (Jan 1–11) and Snake Days (Mi no Hi), and is meant to be kept permanently in one's wallet rather than returned after a year.

The 7-Step Zeniarai Ritual

Hebikubo Shrine is renowned for its Zeniarai (money washing) ritual. Follow these precise mechanics to circulate wealth:

  1. Acquire the Shirohebi Tanesen (seed coin) at the shrine office.
  2. Place the Tanesen on the stone mill (ishi-usu) cup and turn it clockwise 3 times (Zeni-Mawashi).
  3. Place the Tanesen and your personal currency/bills into the bamboo basket (zaru).
  4. Dip the basket in Hakuja Shimizu spring water, rotating it clockwise 3 times.
  5. Cross the red Yumemi-bashi bridge and pray at Hakuja Benzaitensha.
  6. Wipe the currency dry; keep the Tanesen in your wallet and spend or invest the washed money to circulate wealth.
  7. On future visits, return the old Tanesen to the connecting rod (Zeni-tsunagi) before receiving a new one.

Crowd Dynamics & Best Times to Visit

On Mi no Hi and Tsuchinoto-Mi no Hi (Earth Snake Days), peak wait times range from 2 to 4 hours (and up to 8 hours during New Year crossovers). Early morning visits between 7:00 AM and 9:00 AM are highly recommended to avoid queues.
