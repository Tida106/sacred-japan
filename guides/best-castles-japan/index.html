The Best Castles in Japan, Ranked: 30 Castles in Photos

日本の名城
WRITTEN BY SACRED JAPAN ／ LICENSED NATIONAL TOUR GUIDE (ENGLISH)
Home › Guides › Best Castles in Japan

Which castle should you see first? This page puts the most visited castles in order, each with its best photo, and links to a full guide for every one. Photos mostly show the keep or the main gate, as it looks from the usual viewpoint.

How the ranking works: places 1 to 10 follow NAVITIME’s 2025 data on which castles visitors from the Americas, Europe and Oceania searched for most (Travel Voice, April 2026). Places 11 onward are my own order, starting with the castles that rank highly among Asian visitors in the same data. Only twelve of these castles still have their original wooden keeps; the others are rebuilt, ruins or palaces, and each card says which.

The Top Three

1

Rebuilt keep

Osaka Castle 大阪城

Hideyoshi’s castle, with a 1931 concrete keep, an observation deck and a moat you can walk around.

Read the Osaka guide

Photo: Otto Domes, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

2

Palace

Nijo Castle 二条城

Ieyasu’s Kyoto palace of 1603, with nightingale floors and gold-leaf rooms. There is no keep here, and that is the point.

Read the Nijo guide

Photo: 京都束, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

3

Original keep

Himeji Castle 姫路城

The White Heron: the best known of Japan’s twelve original keeps, and a maze of walls and gates.

Read the Himeji guide

Photo: Bernard Gagnon, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Places 4 to 10

4

Ruins

Edo Castle 江戸城

The shogun’s seat, now the free East Gardens of the Imperial Palace. The keep was lost in the great fire of 1657.

Read the Edo guide

Photo: Another Believer, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

5

Rebuilt turrets

Kanazawa Castle 金沢城

Lead-tile turrets and an original gate of 1788, right beside Kenrokuen garden.

Read the Kanazawa guide

Photo: Zairon, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

6

Rebuilt keep

Nagoya Castle 名古屋城

Golden shachi on the roof and a rebuilt palace of gold-leaf rooms. The keep itself has been closed since May 2018.

Read the Nagoya guide

Photo: Noodle snacks (Base64 edit), CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

7

Rebuilt keep

Hiroshima Castle 広島城

The Carp Castle: brought down by the 1945 blast and rebuilt in 1958.

Read the Hiroshima guide

Photo: Marion & Christoph Aistleitner, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

8

Rebuilt keep

Kumamoto Castle 熊本城

Famous for its curving stone walls. Damaged in 2016 and again in July 2026, so check what is open.

Read the Kumamoto guide

Photo: Hyppolyte de Saint-Rambert, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

9

Original keep

Matsumoto Castle 松本城

A black keep over a moat, with a red bridge and the Alps behind it.

Read the Matsumoto guide

Photo: Manishprabhune, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

10

Rebuilt keep

Odawara Castle 小田原城

The Hojo stronghold, with a 1960 keep, a ninja museum and cherry trees in spring.

Read the Odawara guide

Photo: 663highland, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons (resized)

Original keep

Date Masamune’s hilltop fort. There is no keep, but there are huge walls and a view over the city.

Original keep

Kochi Castle 高知城

The keep and the main hall both survive, which is rare.

Original keep

Original keep

Original keep

Ruins

Two More Guides, Without Photos Yet

We also have guides to Wakayama Castle, the Kishu Tokugawa seat with a slanted covered bridge, and Tsuyama Castle, known for its stone walls and about a thousand cherry trees.

Which Castle Should You Visit?

  • One famous castle, with the least effort: Himeji.
  • A palace rather than a tower: Nijo in Kyoto, or Kochi, where the palace also survives.
  • A half day from a big city: Inuyama from Nagoya, or Hikone from Kyoto.
  • A hike to a keep: Bitchu Matsuyama, on a mountaintop.
  • Cherry blossoms: Hirosaki and Takato.
  • Walls and history: Kumamoto (check what is open) and Marugame.
