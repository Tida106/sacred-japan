Tofuku-ji: The Temple Named After Two Others

東福寺　通天橋の紅葉

A Kyoto Zen temple whose name borrows from two great Nara temples, and whose maple valley is among the most photographed autumn views in Japan.

As a licensed guide, I like temples with a good naming story, and Tofuku-ji has one. The name is a mash-up: To from Todai-ji and fuku from Kofuku-ji, the two great temples of Nara. When the imperial chancellor Kujo Michiie founded it in 1236, with the monk Enni as its first head priest, the ambition was written right into the name: a temple in Kyoto as grand as those two.

Enni had studied Zen in China, and he is also believed to have helped bring udon noodles to Japan, which is a nice detail to remember when you stop for lunch afterward. Today Tofuku-ji is the head temple of the Tofuku-ji branch of Rinzai Zen and ranks as one of the Kyoto Gozan, the "Five Mountains" of Kyoto Zen temples. And for a few weeks each autumn, it is the most crowded temple in the city, for one very good reason.

Tsutenkyo: A Bridge Over a Valley of Maples通天橋

The reason is the Tsutenkyo Bridge, a covered wooden walkway about 100 meters long that crosses a ravine packed with Japanese maples. Visitors have come to look at these leaves for generations, and the woodblock artist Hiroshige drew the scene. Stand on the bridge in the third week of November and the valley is a bowl of red and orange below you; look back from the far side and the bridge itself seems to float above it.

Two smaller bridges, Engetsukyo and Gaunkyo, cross the same valley as the famous one. Beyond the bridge you reach Kaisando Hall, the mausoleum of the temple's first head priest, where a stone path runs between a dry rock garden on one side and a pond garden on the other. It is a calm ending to a crowded walk.

🍁 The guide's tip: Photograph the bridge from the side, not only from it. The bridge is where everyone stands, and the maples themselves are the subject. Give yourself time to stop at the edges of the crowd.

The Sanmon, and a Temple That Burned三門

Before the maples, look up. The Sanmon, the temple's main gate, was built in 1425, stands about 22 meters tall, and is described as the oldest Zen main gate in Japan. It is a National Treasure, and you can admire it freely from the road, without a ticket.

Much around it is younger. Tofuku-ji burned in the 14th century, and after a fire in 1319 the temple even sent a merchant ship to Yuan China to bring back materials for the rebuilding. The ship sank on the return voyage. In the Meiji period, reforms shrank the temple from about 70 buildings to 25, and a fire in 1881 destroyed the main hall, the Hojo and the lecture hall along with the Shakyamuni statue; the main hall and the Hatto were rebuilt in 1917. What you see is a survivor, and a rebuilt one.

The Hojo Garden: A Modern Surprise方丈庭園

Most visitors rush to the bridge and miss the quieter ticket. The gardens wrapped around the Hojo, the former head priest's residence, were redesigned in the late 1930s by Mirei Shigemori, a garden designer who blended traditional Japanese principles with modern taste. Each side has its own personality: on the north, a checkerboard of moss and square stones; on the south, raked gravel that suggests waves; on the west, cylindrical stones representing the Seven Mountains and Eight Seas; and on the east, a simple composition of moss and rocks.

It is one of the best places in Kyoto to see how a 20th-century designer could use an ancient vocabulary, and it is usually calmer than the bridge. If you only have time for one paid area, I would choose the bridge in peak season, but I would not skip the garden if you have any time left.

When to Go, and How to Beat the Crowds見頃と混雑対策

The best viewing period usually runs from mid-November to early December, with peak colour most often in mid to late November. Colours change from year to year, so check a current foliage report shortly before you go. At peak, the bridge is extremely crowded.

  • Arrive at opening. In late autumn the temple opens around 8:30, earlier than at other times of the year. Be at the gate before then.
  • Go on a weekday. Weekends in the second half of November are the hardest days.
  • Expect an autumn price. During the foliage season the Tsutenkyo and Kaisando ticket rises, and the combination ticket is not offered.
  • Want fewer people? Our guide to autumn sanctuaries is for travelers who would rather earn a quieter autumn.

Practical Guide for Visitors実践ガイド

Getting there
Tofukuji Station is one stop, about 2 minutes, from Kyoto Station on the JR Nara Line, then about a 10-minute walk. The Keihan Main Line also stops at Tofukuji Station. The next station on the JR Nara Line is Inari, for Fushimi Inari, so the two pair neatly in one day.
Fees
Normally about 500 yen for the Hojo garden and about 600 yen for Tsutenkyo Bridge and Kaisando Hall. In autumn the Tsutenkyo ticket rises to about 1,000 yen. Confirm current prices and dates before you go.
Hours
Hours change by season. Late autumn opens earlier than the rest of the year, and last admission comes before closing. Check the official site for the current schedule.

Frequently Asked Questionsよくあるご質問

When is the best time to see autumn leaves at Tofuku-ji?
Usually mid-November to early December, with peak colour most often in mid to late November. Colours shift every year, so check a current foliage report shortly before you travel. Tofuku-ji is crowded at peak, so go at opening time on a weekday if you can.
How much does Tofuku-ji cost?
Entry to the grounds and the Sanmon gate is free to look at. Normally, the Hojo garden costs about 500 yen and the Tsutenkyo Bridge and Kaisando Hall about 600 yen. During the autumn season the Tsutenkyo ticket rises to about 1,000 yen and the combination ticket is not offered. Prices and dates change, so confirm on the official site.
How do I get to Tofuku-ji from Kyoto Station?
Take the JR Nara Line one stop, about 2 minutes, to Tofukuji Station, then walk about 10 minutes. Tofukuji Station is also on the Keihan Main Line. Kyoto City Bus 208 from Kyoto Station is another option.
Why is it called Tofuku-ji?
The name joins one character from each of two great Nara temples, Todai-ji and Kofuku-ji. The temple was founded in 1236 by the imperial chancellor Kujo Michiie, with the monk Enni as its first head priest, and the aim was a temple in Kyoto as grand as those two.
Is Tofuku-ji worth visiting outside autumn?
Yes. The Hojo gardens, designed by Mirei Shigemori in the late 1930s, are among the most striking modern gardens in Kyoto, and the temple is far quieter in other seasons. The Sanmon gate, built in 1425, is also worth seeing at any time of year.

After the Maplesむすびに

Tofuku-ji is famous for one view, and it earns it. But the temple is also a story about ambition, fire and rebuilding, and a modern garden hidden behind a very old gate. Go for the maples, then leave yourself an hour for everything else.
