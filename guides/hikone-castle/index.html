Hikone Castle: The Ii Clan's Keep on Lake Biwa

彦根城

A National Treasure keep that came from another castle, a castle that escaped demolition, and a lakeside garden.

WRITTEN BY SACRED JAPAN ／ LICENSED NATIONAL TOUR GUIDE (ENGLISH)

As a licensed guide, I often suggest Hikone Castle to guests who have seen Kyoto and want one more National Treasure without a long trip. It sits on the shore of Lake Biwa in Shiga, about 50 minutes from Kyoto by train. It is one of Japan's twelve original castles, its three-story keep is a National Treasure, and the keep, according to Wikipedia, was moved here from another castle.

This guide covers who built it, how the keep arrived, the story of how it escaped demolition, Genkyuen garden, Hikonyan, and what to know before you go.

Ii Naomasa and the Move to the Lake歴史

Wikipedia credits Ii Naomasa, a general of Tokugawa Ieyasu, with starting the castle. He moved his seat from the hilltop Sawayama Castle, about two kilometers away, to a new site on the shore of Lake Biwa. Naomasa died in 1602, just as work had begun, and his son Ii Naokatsu completed it in 1622, according to Wikipedia. japan-guide says construction took about 20 years.

The Ii clan held the castle until the end of the feudal age in 1868. For the other surviving keeps, see my castles overview and The Best Castles in Japan.

A Keep That Came From Elsewhere天守

The keep is three stories high, with attached turrets. Wikipedia says it was moved from Otsu Castle, where it was originally built in 1575 as a five-story tower and later reduced in height. I find this a fun detail for guests, because it means you are looking at a keep that had a first life somewhere else.

Other buildings are worth a look on the way up. Wikipedia lists the Tenbin Yagura gate, brought from Nagahama Castle, the Taikomon Yagura (Drum Gate) and the stable, all Important Cultural Properties. The stable is a rare survivor, because most castles lost theirs. japan-guide notes that most of the inner moats, walls, guard houses and gates also survive, and the keep was designated a National Treasure in 1952, per Wikipedia.

How the Castle Was Saved保存

After the fall of the shogunate, the new government ordered feudal fortifications to be dismantled. According to Wikipedia, Hikone was spared after the emperor personally requested it while touring the area. I would call that a story to tell on the spot, since without it there would be no keep to climb.

Another name linked to the castle is Ii Naosuke, who, as tairo, was the virtual ruler of Japan in the last years of the shogunate and was assassinated in 1860, per Wikipedia. The Ii family is a big part of what makes this castle feel different from Himeji or Matsumoto, because the history here is political as well as military.

Genkyuen and Hikonyan庭園

At the foot of the castle, just outside the inner moat, is Genkyuen, a daimyo garden. The city tourism site describes it as northeast of the castle, with nine bridges over islands and coves, and recommends a stroll and a bowl of matcha. It is included in the castle ticket, per japan-guide.

You may also meet Hikonyan, the castle mascot. Wikipedia says it is based on a maneki-neko folktale about Ii Naotaka. Children love it, and it is a good photo stop.

The town beside the castle is also worth an hour. The city site lists Yume-Kyobashi Castle Road, which keeps an Edo-period look with white walls and black lattice, and suggests having lunch there.

Visiting Hikone Castleアクセス

  • Getting there: The city tourism site says Hikone is about 50 minutes from Kyoto and 80 minutes from Osaka. From Hikone Station, japan-guide lists a 15-minute walk along the main road.
  • Hours: 8:30 to 17:00, with last entry at 16:30, with no closing days for the castle, per japan-guide. The museum closes December 25 to 31 and on a few other dates.
  • Admission: japan-guide lists 1,000 yen for the castle and garden, 1,500 yen with the museum, and 700 yen for the museum alone.
  • Seasons: japan-guide says cherry blossoms are usually at full bloom in early to mid April, about a week later than in Kyoto.

Prices and hours change, so please confirm on the official Hikone tourism site before you go.

Making It a Day Trip滞在

Hikone pairs well with Kyoto or Osaka in a single day, and the lake gives the trip a different feel from the temple circuit.

Frequently Asked Questionsよくあるご質問

How much does Hikone Castle cost?
japan-guide lists 1,000 yen for the castle and garden, 1,500 yen with the museum, and 700 yen for the museum alone. Please confirm on the official site, because prices change.
Is Hikone Castle a National Treasure?
Yes. Its keep is one of five designated National Treasures, along with Himeji, Matsumoto, Inuyama and Matsue.
How far is Hikone Castle from Kyoto?
The city tourism site says about 50 minutes by train, followed by a walk of about 15 minutes from Hikone Station.
Was the Hikone keep moved from another castle?
Wikipedia says yes: it was moved from Otsu Castle, where it was built in 1575 as a five-story tower and later reduced in height.
What is Hikonyan?
The castle's mascot. Wikipedia says it is based on a maneki-neko folktale about Ii Naotaka.

The Castle That Was Nearly Pulled Downむすびに

Hikone is the castle I suggest when a guest wants the history without the crowds of Himeji. It has a keep with a past life, a garden by the water, and a mascot cat.
