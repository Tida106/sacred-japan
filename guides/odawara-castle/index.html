Odawara Castle: The Hojo Stronghold, a 1960 Keep and the Ninja Museum

小田原城

A 1960 reconstructed keep on the site of the Later Hojo stronghold in Odawara, ten minutes on foot from the station.

WRITTEN BY SACRED JAPAN ／ LICENSED NATIONAL TOUR GUIDE (ENGLISH)

As a licensed guide, I tell visitors that Odawara Castle is the one most people see from the Shinkansen window and then forget about. That is a mistake. This was the headquarters of the Later Hojo clan, who ruled the Kanto region from here for about a century, and it fell to Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1590. The keep you climb today is a reproduction, but the story under it is not.

This guide covers the history, what is actually inside the keep and the two small museums, the cherry blossoms, and the practical details. Where my sources disagree, I say so, and I mark claims I found in only one place.

The Hojo, Hideyoshi and 1590北条氏と小田原攻め

Jeepe says the site began as a fortress built by the Omori clan in the mid-15th century, and that Hojo Soun took it around 1500. The city's English page agrees that an Omori predecessor stood here and that it became the seat of the Odawara Hojo in the Warring States period. Jeepe puts the Later Hojo rule at about 100 years across five generations.

In 1590 Toyotomi Hideyoshi attacked and defeated the Hojo (japan-guide). Jeepe adds that an outer perimeter of about 9 kilometers was built during the siege. I have not seen the outcome of the siege detailed in these sources, so I will leave the story there and point you to the exhibits.

In the Edo period, Jeepe says the lords were the Okubo and Inaba clans; japan-guide does not mention them, so treat that as a single source. japan-guide says an earthquake destroyed the castle in 1703 and it was quickly rebuilt, and that in 1870 the keep and other buildings were dismantled and sold.

The Keep and the Museums天守と博物館

The keep was rebuilt in ferro-concrete in 1960, based on Edo-period models and drawings (japan-guide); Jeepe also gives 1960. It looks three stories tall from the outside and has four inside (japan-guide, the city page). The exhibits cover the castle’s history, armor and swords, and the top floor has views over the park and the city.

The photographer writes on Commons that he kept his friends waiting just to shoot a sunset shot of the castle. The colors look strongly processed, with a more intense sky than you will usually see, so please treat it as a mood picture rather than a guide to the real light. What it does show clearly is the shape: a stone base, then a white keep with several layers of tiled roofs and triangular gables. The windows are lit because it is evening, but I have no source for evening opening, so check the hours above.

Commons has two files of the keep from 14 April 2018 by this photographer, one from the southeast and one from the east, and I believe this is the southeast view, though I cannot match the file to the time stamp with certainty. Standing this low you can see why the keep feels taller than its modest size. The stone base rises in a gentle curve, and each roof level has its own gable. A small viewing gallery runs around the upper floor, which fits the top-floor views described above.

This is the other file from the same day, which I take to be the east view. The camera is close to the stone base and looks up, so the roofs stack above the gable at the entrance level. Under the grey sky the white walls and dark tiles stand out clearly. A few people are standing at the opening in the base, which gives a rough sense of scale. Compared with the sunny view from the southeast, this is the moodier way to see it.

Commons titles this “Odawara Castle in 2024.” A big pine leans in from the left, bare branches frame the right side, and the keep sits on its stone base against a blue sky streaked with cloud. A few visitors are crossing the open ground below. It is a good reminder that this is a place for a winter walk as well as the cherry season. I cannot tell which species the trees on the right are, so please treat them as scenery.

Two smaller museums sit nearby. The Samurai Museum is in the Tokiwaki Gate building, across from the keep, with armor and swords (japan-guide). The Ninja Museum is near the main entrance and has an obstacle course where you can try ninja skills (japan-guide). Jeepe says it is themed on the Fuma ninja, who supported the Hojo, and that you can try shuriken throwing. Jeepe also lists an armor-dressing experience for 500 yen (single source; please check the hours).

Commons titles this as the Tokiwagi Gate, taken on 7 June 2015. This is the building that japan-guide, which spells it Tokiwaki, says holds the Samurai Museum, so it is the gate you pass or use on the way to the keep. A dark lattice window sits above the passage, and visitors on the stairs give a sense of scale. I have not checked which entrance is used for the museum today, so please follow the signs on site.

Gates, Moat and Cherry Blossoms城門と桜

japan-guide says the three major gates leading to the castle were restored in the years after the keep, and the grounds are a park with a moat. Jeepe describes the area as the castle ruins park and says it is a ten-minute walk from the station.

This is the Akagane (copper) Gate, one of the main gates into the castle, photographed on 7 June 2015. The doors are made of tall wooden slats with rows of metal studs, set between thick stone walls. Through the passage you can see green trees beyond. It is a good place to look at how a castle gate is built to slow an attacker down.

The Japanese description on Commons calls this the Akagane Gate, the front gate of the Ninomaru enclosure, and says it was restored in 1997 (Heisei 9). That fits what japan-guide says about the main gates being restored in the years after the keep. The photo was taken on 1 February 2008 and is a small file (1,280 pixels wide), so it is a little soft.

Commons gives this as the Akagane Gate seen from the southeast. The wooden sign at the right reads Akaganemon with opening hours that I read as 7:00 am to 9:00 pm and a lighting time from sunset until 10:00 pm. That sign is from 2018 and I have not seen a current one, so please check the hours on the day. The tall stone wall and the moat show how the gate was meant to be reached only along a narrow route.

Commons titles this the Umadashi Gate seen from the east, taken on 14 April 2018. The white walls on both sides have small square and triangular openings, which are loopholes for defenders. The gate itself is plain, with a simple tiled roof, and the yard in front is open gravel. A wooden sign stands at the left. Compared with the big Akagane Gate, it is a much quieter corner of the grounds.

Commons titles this only “Odawara Castle Gate,” so I do not name which gate it is. Low early light warms the white wall, a wooden bridge crosses the water at the left, and a tall pine rises behind the tiled roof. The stone base is tall, with the moat at its foot. It is a good example of how the castle is built from gate, wall and water working together.

The Chinese caption on Commons says this is the entrance of Odawara Castle in July 2018. It shows a small tiled gate between white walls, with another roof and wall visible beyond it. The decorated end tiles on the ridge are worth a closer look. I do not know which named gate this is, so I describe only what the caption says.

The Japanese caption on Commons calls this the Sumiyoshi Bridge, which spans the moat at the Akagane Gate, photographed on 17 March 2020. The orange cones on the bridge suggest some kind of work or control when it was taken, and I do not know what for. The moat water and the stone walls give a good idea of the setting. The still water also reflects the white tower above.

For flowers, japan-guide lists cherry blossoms in late March to early April, plus plum, azalea, wisteria, iris, hydrangea and lotus at other times. Jeepe counts about 300 Somei Yoshino trees, a place on Japan’s Top 100 cherry blossom spots, and illuminations during the season. The city page also names plum, cherry and hydrangea.

Commons gives this as the castle taken on 1 April 2018, and the crowd and the food stalls suggest a blossom weekend, with people on the viewing gallery at the top of the keep. The cherry tree is in full flower and its white-pink color looks lovely against the white walls. This is one day, so I would not read the date as a promise for any year. If you want a quiet visit, the park is probably better on a weekday morning, but that is my suggestion, not a fact from a source.

The caption on Commons is just Odawara Castle, taken on 4 April 2009. From the height it looks to have been taken from the keep, though that is my reading and not something the file says. Cherry trees in bloom fill the foreground, and beyond the town you can see the sea and the hills along the coast. It fits japan-guide’s note that the top floor has views over the park and the city.

Visiting Odawara Castleアクセス

  • Admission (adult): keep 510 yen, Ninja Museum 310 yen, Samurai Museum 200 yen; keep plus both museums 610 yen (japan-guide). Jeepe lists a keep plus Samurai Museum combo at 610 yen, so please confirm the bundle at the gate.
  • Hours: 9:00 to 17:00, last entry 16:30 (japan-guide, Jeepe, city page).
  • Closed: keep on the second Wednesday of December, 31 December and 1 January; museums on 31 December and 1 January only (japan-guide).
  • From Odawara Station: about 10 minutes on foot from the East Exit (japan-guide, Jeepe).
  • From Tokyo: about 35 minutes from Tokyo Station by Shinkansen, about 75 minutes from Shinjuku on the Odakyu Romancecar, about 50 minutes from Yokohama on the JR Tokaido Line (Jeepe). The same article elsewhere gives slightly different times, so check a timetable.
  • Time needed: 20 to 40 minutes for the keep (japan-guide); 1.5 to 2 hours for the whole park with both museums (Jeepe).
  • By car: about 10 minutes from the Ogikubo Interchange on the Odawara-Atsugi Road (Jeepe).

Making It a Trip滞在

Odawara is the gateway to Hakone, so most visitors fit the castle into a half day before the mountains. If you like castles that have a missing keep, our Edo Castle guide covers the Imperial Palace East Gardens, and our Nagoya Castle guide covers another rebuilt keep.

Frequently Asked Questionsよくあるご質問

How much does it cost to enter Odawara Castle?
The main keep is 510 yen for adults and 200 yen for elementary and junior high students (japan-guide, Jeepe). The Ninja Museum is 310 yen and the Samurai Museum 200 yen, and a keep-plus-both-museums ticket is listed at 610 yen by japan-guide. Jeepe lists a keep-plus-Samurai-Museum combo at 610 yen, so the exact bundle differs between sources. The city's older English page shows 500 yen for the keep, which looks out of date. Please confirm at the gate or on the official site.
What are the opening hours of Odawara Castle?
The keep and the museums are open 9:00 to 17:00, with last entry at 16:30 (japan-guide, Jeepe, the city page). The keep is closed on the second Wednesday of December and on 31 December and 1 January; the museums close only on 31 December and 1 January (japan-guide).
How do I get to Odawara Castle from Tokyo?
Odawara Station is about 35 minutes from Tokyo Station by Shinkansen (Jeepe), or about 75 minutes from Shinjuku on the Odakyu Romancecar (Jeepe; the same article also says about 1 hour 20 minutes elsewhere). From the station's East Exit the castle is a walk of about 10 minutes (japan-guide, Jeepe).
How long should I spend at Odawara Castle?
japan-guide suggests 20 to 40 minutes for the keep itself, and Jeepe suggests 30 minutes to 1 hour for the keep, or 1.5 to 2 hours for the whole park with both museums. Add more time in cherry blossom season.
Is Odawara Castle an original castle?
The keep is not. It is a ferro-concrete reproduction built in 1960 from Edo-period drawings and models (japan-guide, Jeepe). The keep and other buildings were dismantled and sold in 1870 (japan-guide). The three main gates were restored in the following years.
When are the cherry blossoms at Odawara Castle?
Late March to early April (japan-guide, Jeepe). Jeepe counts about 300 Somei Yoshino trees, says the park is on Japan's Top 100 cherry blossom list, and says there are evening illuminations during the season. Dates move with the weather.
Can I combine Odawara Castle with Hakone?
Yes. Odawara is the usual gateway to Hakone, and both japan-guide and Jeepe suggest pairing them. The castle works well as a first stop before heading up into the mountains.

Before You Board the Hakone Trainむすびに

Give Odawara Castle an hour on your way to Hakone. Climb the keep for the view, try the ninja course if you have children with you, and remember that the keep is young but the site is not.

⛩️

The keep is new. The stronghold under it ruled a region.
