Japan Travel Planning: The 2026 Checklist

日本旅行の計画ガイド

Days, seasons, the new JR Pass prices, and the November tax-free changes — everything to settle before you book

Every year a handful of small rules change — a pass gets more expensive, a tax refund process gets rebuilt, a transit card runs short — and travelers who planned their trip a year ago on outdated advice find out the hard way at the airport. This guide is deliberately dated: it reflects what is true as of October 2026, including two changes that take effect this year and will directly affect your budget and your paperwork.

As a licensed guide, I write the itinerary logistics the same way I'd brief a client before their flight — the decisions that actually matter, in the order you need to make them, with the fluff left out.

How Many Days Do You Actually Need?必要な旅行日数

This is the question that shapes every other decision, so settle it first.

  • 7 days or fewer: Stay in one region. Tokyo plus day trips (Nikko, Kamakura, Hakone) or Kyoto plus day trips (Nara, Osaka) both work well. Trying to add a third city in under a week usually means spending more time on trains than at your destinations.
  • 10–14 days: The sweet spot for a first trip. Tokyo, Kyoto, and one more stop — Hiroshima, Osaka, or a mountain detour like Koyasan — fit comfortably without rushing.
  • 3 weeks or more: Worth considering a slower pace in fewer places, or adding a region most first-timers skip, such as Tohoku, Shikoku, or Kyushu.

A common mistake is building a itinerary around a long checklist of cities rather than a shorter list of experiences — a sunrise at a shrine, a night in a temple, a single unhurried afternoon in a garden. Trips built the second way are remembered longer.

When to Go訪れる季節

SPRING

March – April

桜の季節

Cherry blossoms, mild weather, the single most popular season. Book accommodation 3–6 months out, especially late March to early April in Kyoto and Tokyo.

SUMMER

June – August

夏と祭り

Hot and humid, but festival season — fireworks displays and matsuri throughout the country. Fewer crowds at sacred sites, more at beaches and mountains.

AUTUMN

October – late Nov.

紅葉の季節

Comfortable temperatures and foliage that rivals spring's blossoms, especially in Kyoto and Nikko by mid-to-late November. The second peak season — book just as early.

WINTER

December – February

静かな冬

Cold but quiet. The best season for an unhurried, uncrowded temple visit — Koyasan under snow is unforgettable — and for New Year's hatsumode traditions.

Entry Basics: Visa and Visit Japan Web入国の準備

Travelers from many countries — including the US, UK, most EU nations, Canada, and Australia — can enter Japan visa-free for short tourist stays. Requirements do change, though, so confirm your specific passport's current status before booking flights rather than assuming last year's rule still applies.

Before you fly, registering on Visit Japan Web lets you complete immigration, customs, and (if relevant) tax-free declarations online in advance, which speeds up arrival significantly. It takes about fifteen minutes and is worth doing a few days before departure.

Money, IC Cards, and the New Tax-Free Rulesお金とICカード

Japan remains more cash-reliant than many visitors expect, especially outside major cities and at smaller temples, shukubo, and family-run restaurants. Carry cash as your default, and treat cards as a bonus.

For transit and convenience-store payments, a rechargeable IC card is essential. If you carry an iPhone, the easiest option is Welcome Suica in Apple Wallet — no deposit, foreign credit cards accepted, valid 180 days. Android users should instead buy a physical Welcome Suica or Tourist PASMO card on arrival at Narita or Haneda, since most Android phones sold outside Japan lack the chip Japanese transit readers require. Both tourist cards are non-refundable, so don't over-load them.

!

Tax-Free Shopping Changes From November 1, 2026

Japan is switching from tax-exempt in-store purchases to a refund-based system. You will pay the full, tax-inclusive price at the register, then claim the 10% consumption tax back at the airport within 90 days of purchase — not have it waived on the spot as before. Keep every receipt: if even one item from a tax-free receipt is missing at departure, the whole receipt's refund is void. Build in extra airport time for the confirmation step, and expect credit-card refunds to take 1–2 weeks, bank transfers 2–4 weeks.

Getting Around: Is the JR Pass Still Worth It?JRパスは必要か

Nationwide Japan Rail Pass prices rose on October 1, 2026 — the 7-day Ordinary pass went from ¥50,000 to ¥53,000, the 14-day from ¥80,000 to ¥84,000, and the 21-day from ¥100,000 to ¥105,000. This follows an earlier increase in 2023, and the pass's math has changed as a result.

A rough rule that still holds: the JR Pass pays for itself if your trip includes at least two or three long-distance Shinkansen legs — for example Tokyo → Kyoto → Hiroshima → Tokyo. A single round trip, such as just Tokyo to Kyoto and back, is usually cheaper bought as individual tickets. For a trip confined to one region, a regional rail pass (JR West, JR East, or a Kansai-area pass) is typically the better value, and city subway/bus fares are unaffected by any of this either way.

Building an Itinerary Around Sacred Sites聖地を軸にした旅程

Shrines and temples reward slow mornings more than any other kind of sightseeing — most are at their best (and least crowded) in the first hour after opening, before tour groups arrive. A few practical pairings that work well geographically:

  • Tokyo side: Meiji Jingu and Senso-ji fit easily into a city day; Nikko Toshogu makes a strong full-day trip north.
  • Kyoto side: Fushimi Inari at dawn, Kiyomizu-dera and Kinkaku-ji through the day, then a quieter garden temple like Ryoan-ji before sunset.
  • Add a night that isn't a hotel: A single overnight at a shukubo — Koyasan is the easiest first choice — reshapes the rhythm of an entire trip. See our temple stay guide for how to book one.

A Short, Honest Packing List持ち物リスト

01

Comfortable, Slip-On-Friendly Shoes

Many temples require removing shoes, and you'll walk far more than you expect. Slip-ons save real time at busy sites.

02

A Coin Purse

Japan still uses coins heavily, and temple offering boxes, vending machines, and small shops all favor exact change.

03

A Compact Portable Charger

Between navigation, translation apps, and photos, your phone will work harder on this trip than almost any other.

04

A Small Folder for Receipts

With the tax-free system changing in November 2026, keeping every receipt organized until you clear customs at departure matters more than it used to.

Frequently Asked Questionsよくあるご質問

How many days do I need in Japan?
10 to 14 days covers Tokyo, Kyoto, and one side trip comfortably without rushing. A first trip of under 7 days works best focused on a single region, such as Tokyo and nearby day trips, rather than spreading thin across the whole country.
Is the Japan Rail Pass still worth it in 2026?
It depends on your route. Nationwide JR Pass prices rose on October 1, 2026 (the 7-day Ordinary pass went from ¥50,000 to ¥53,000), so it now pays off mainly on trips with at least two or three long-distance bullet train legs, such as Tokyo–Kyoto–Hiroshima. For a trip confined to one region, a regional rail pass or paying per-ride is usually cheaper.
What changed with tax-free shopping in Japan in 2026?
From November 1, 2026, Japan moves to a refund-based tax-free system. You pay the full tax-inclusive price in-store, then claim the 10% consumption tax back at the airport within 90 days of purchase, instead of having it exempted at the register. Keep every receipt, since a missing item invalidates the whole receipt's refund.
Do I need a visa to visit Japan?
Travelers from many countries, including the US, UK, EU nations, Canada, and Australia, can enter visa-free for short tourist stays. Always confirm current requirements for your specific passport before booking, as visa waiver agreements can change.
Should I get a physical Suica or PASMO, or use my phone?
If you have an iPhone, Welcome Suica in Apple Wallet is usually the easiest option: no deposit, foreign credit cards accepted, valid 180 days. Android users should buy a physical Welcome Suica or Tourist PASMO on arrival instead, since most Android phones sold outside Japan lack the required chip.
When is the best time to visit Japan for shrines and temples?
Early-to-mid autumn (October to late November) and early spring (March to April) offer the best balance of mild weather, seasonal color, and festival activity at sacred sites, though both are peak season with the biggest crowds. Winter offers the quietest temple visits, including Koyasan.

Start Planning旅の始まり

Get the logistics settled early — days, season, pass or no pass, and now the tax-free paperwork — and the rest of the trip takes care of itself. Once the framework is in place, the real planning is just choosing which of Japan's sacred sites to build your days around.

Sources verified at time of writing (October 2026). Prices, visa rules, and tax-free procedures can change; always confirm current details with official sources before you travel.
