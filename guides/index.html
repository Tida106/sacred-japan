Guides to Japan's Sacred Sites & Culture

神社と寺院をめぐる完全ガイド

Long-form English guides written for international visitors — by a Japanese national licensed as a National Tour Guide in art and culture.

WRITTEN BY SACRED JAPAN ／ LICENSED NATIONAL TOUR GUIDE (ENGLISH)
Home › Guides

Welcome to the Sacred Japan Guides — a growing library of deep-dive English guides to Japan's most sacred places and the cultural practices that surround them. Use them to plan a pilgrimage to the holiest shrines and temples, to learn the etiquette before you arrive, and to find quieter reasons to fall in love with the country.

Sacred Places to Visit 聖地を訪ねる
GUIDE 08 ／ 伊勢神宮
⛩️ Ise Grand Shrine: Japan's Most Sacred Place →
Japan's holiest shrine — Naiku and Geku, the proper pilgrimage order, the 20-year rebuilding, and how to plan a respectful visit.
GUIDE 09 ／ 熱田神宮
⚔️ Atsuta Shrine: The Sacred Sword →
Japan's second-holiest shrine — home of the Kusanagi sword, the Nobunaga-bei wall, and a 1,000-year-old camphor tree hidden in a Nagoya forest.
GUIDE 07 ／ 三種の神器
🔱 The Three Sacred Treasures of Japan →
The mirror, sword, and jewel no one is allowed to see — Japan's Imperial Regalia explained, plus the shrines (Ise, Atsuta) you can actually visit.
GUIDE 03 ／ 伏見稲荷
📖 Best Time to Visit Fushimi Inari →
Beat the crowds at Kyoto's most photographed shrine — hour-by-hour, season-by-season, FAQ.
GUIDE 06 ／ 高野山
⛩️ A Night on the Sacred Mountain: Koyasan Temple Stay →
Sleep inside a working Buddhist temple, eat shojin ryori, and walk Okunoin's lantern-lit path to Kobo Daishi's mausoleum — the complete shukubo guide.
GUIDE 10 ／ 出雲大社
⛩️ Izumo Taisha: The Shrine of Marriage & the Gathering of the Gods →
Japan's shrine of enmusubi (bonds) — Okuninushi, the annual gathering of all 8 million gods, the 2-bow-4-clap etiquette, and the colossal shimenawa.
GUIDE 11 ／ 春日大社
⛩️ Kasuga Taisha: Nara's Shrine of 3,000 Lanterns & Sacred Deer →
Nara's UNESCO World Heritage shrine — 3,000 stone and bronze lanterns, the sacred Nara deer, the Fujiwara clan, and the Mantōrō festivals.
GUIDE 12 ／ 厳島神社
⛩️ Itsukushima Shrine: The Floating Torii Gate of Miyajima →
Japan's iconic floating torii — UNESCO World Heritage Site, the sacred reason it sits in the sea, Taira no Kiyomori's Heian masterpiece, and high tide vs. low tide planning.
GUIDE 13 ／ 明治神宮
⛩️ Meiji Jingu: Tokyo's Sacred Forest and the Emperor Who Opened Japan →
A hundred-year forest engineered to feel eternal, the emperor who navigated Japan's modernization, and three million people at New Year — in the heart of Tokyo.
GUIDE 14 ／ 日光東照宮
⛩️ Nikko Toshogu: The Golden Shrine That Made a Shogun a God →
Gold leaf, 508 carvings, and the Three Wise Monkeys' hidden story — the lavish shrine built to deify Japan's greatest warlord and cement his dynasty's divine rule.
GUIDE 15 ／ 住吉大社
⛩️ Sumiyoshi Taisha: Osaka's Ancient Shrine of the Sea Gods →
One of Japan's oldest shrines — the head of 2,300 Sumiyoshi shrines, the pre-Buddhist Sumiyoshi-zukuri architecture, the steep purifying Sorihashi bridge, and the sea gods of old Osaka.
GUIDE 16 ／ 浅草寺
⛩️ Senso-ji: Tokyo's Oldest Temple and the Golden Kannon Pulled From a River →
Tokyo's oldest temple — the Kaminarimon Thunder Gate, the 300-year-old Nakamise street, a golden Kannon no one is allowed to see, and how to worship at a temple in Asakusa.
GUIDE 17 ／ 清水寺
⛩️ Kiyomizu-dera: Kyoto's Temple of the Famous Wooden Stage →
The nail-free stage jutting 13 meters over a Kyoto hillside, the Otowa waterfall's three streams granting one wish each, and the idiom that every Japanese person knows — all explained.
GUIDE 18 ／ 金閣寺
⛩️ Kinkaku-ji: The Golden Pavilion That Was Burned to the Ground — and Why →
Built by a shogun who unified all Japan, burned by a monk who could not bear its beauty, rebuilt more perfectly than before — Kyoto's golden pavilion and the story behind the photograph.
GUIDE 19 ／ 東大寺
⛩️ Todai-ji: The Great Buddha and the Emperor Who Built It Out of Despair →
The world's largest wooden building, Japan's tallest bronze Buddha, and the 8th-century emperor who asked every person in his country to help build a prayer large enough for a nation in catastrophe.
GUIDE 20 ／ 太宰府天満宮
⛩️ Dazaifu Tenmangu: The Scholar Who Became a God — and the Plum Tree That Followed Him →
Falsely accused, exiled, and deified to appease his vengeful spirit — the head of 12,000 Tenmangu shrines, the flying plum tree, and ten million visitors a year praying before exams.
GUIDE 21 ／ 龍安寺
⛩️ Ryoan-ji: The Rock Garden With the Stone You Cannot See →
Fifteen stones, one always hidden, maker unknown — the world's most famous Zen garden, and what the science of perception says it is actually doing to you.
GUIDE 22 ／ 鶴岡八幡宮
⛩️ Tsurugaoka Hachimangu: Kamakura's Samurai Shrine of War, Art, and Resurrection →
The founding shrine of Japan's warrior government — a shogun's assassination on the stone steps, a millennium-old ginkgo that collapsed and grew back, and an unexpected Le Corbusier connection.
GUIDE 23 ／ 成田山新勝寺
⛩️ Naritasan Shinshoji: The Temple Born From a Curse →
Founded as a 10th-century exorcism to destroy a rebel warlord — and 1,000 years later, Tokyoites still avoid visiting here and Kanda Myojin shrine on the same trip.
GUIDE 24 ／ 川崎大師
⛩️ Kawasaki Daishi: The Temple That Invented Hatsumode →
A fisherman's unlucky year, a shogunal pilgrimage, and a 19th-century train line — the temple whose marketing campaign turned one New Year's visit into Japan's most universal tradition.
GUIDE 25 ／ 大宮氷川神社
⛩️ Omiya Hikawa Shrine: The Shrine That Named a City →
Japan's longest shrine approach, a torii gate donated by Meiji Jingu, and 2,500 years of worship — the head of 280 shrines across the Kanto plain, and the reason a city is called "Great Shrine."
GUIDE 27 ／ 伏見稲荷大社
⛩️ Fushimi Inari Taisha: The Mountain That Built Itself Gate by Gate →
10,000 vermilion torii donated one by one across 13 centuries — the foxes, the founding legend, and why every gate has a name on the back.
GUIDE 28 ／ 高野山
🏔️ Mount Koya: The Mountain Where a Saint Never Died →
Kobo Daishi entered eternal meditation in 835 — his followers have brought him breakfast every morning since. 117 temples, 200,000 graves, and the fires that haven't gone out in 1,100 years.
GUIDE 29 ／ 善光寺
🏛️ Zenko-ji Temple: The Buddha No One Has Seen in 1,400 Years →
Sealed since 654 CE — not the emperor, not the head priest, no one. The closest a visitor gets is a pitch-black tunnel beneath the altar and a hidden iron lock.
GUIDE 30 ／ 延暦寺
🕯️ Enryaku-ji: The Temple That Trained Every Sect, Then Got Burned to the Ground →
Pure Land, Zen, Nichiren — every major Japanese Buddhist school traces its founding to Mount Hiei. In 1571, Nobunaga burned it all. The eternal flame lit in 788 CE never went out.
GUIDE 31 ／ 熊野古道
🌲 Kumano Kodo: Japan's Answer to the Camino de Santiago →
The world's only two UNESCO World Heritage pilgrimage routes — the Kii Peninsula's cedar mountains and the Spanish road to Santiago, formally twinned since 1998. The Dual Pilgrim certificate, the three grand shrines, and how to walk it.
GUIDE 32 ／ 平等院鳳凰堂
🪙 Byodoin Temple: The Building on Japan's Ten-Yen Coin →
The Phoenix Hall has appeared on Japan's ten-yen coin since 1951 — and the building it depicts is a thousand years old, the only surviving structure of the Heian period built to look exactly like paradise.
