Katsuragawa Sokusho Myo-o-in: The Chill of Asceticism

Introduction

Hidden deep within the mountains of Shiga Prefecture, Katsuragawa Sokusho Myo-o-in is a place where the air itself feels different. This is not a garden designed for leisurely strolling; it is a rigorous training ground for Tendai Buddhist monks. Surrounded by dense forests and the rush of the Katsura River, it offers an autumn experience defined by spiritual intensity.

The Trial

The barrier here is profound geographical isolation and an unforgiving climate. Reaching the temple requires a 40-minute bus journey into the remote mountains, where the temperature drops significantly. This geographical friction deters the casual tourist. Enduring the biting cold and the long journey is your initiation—a physical preparation that aligns your body with the austere reality of ascetic practice.

The Reward

Your reward is an encounter with raw, untamed beauty. The autumn leaves here are not carefully pruned; they blaze against the backdrop of ancient, weather-beaten halls and the misty gorge. The tense, crystalline air amplifies the striking reds and yellows, creating a deeply moving, almost austere landscape that commands silence and introspection.

The Pilgrim's Guide

Preparation is key. Dress in heavy layers, as the mountain cold is piercing, especially in late autumn. Check the limited bus schedules carefully, as missing a return bus can be disastrous. Above all, maintain absolute silence and respect the monks' training environment. This is a place for quiet reflection, not casual conversation.
