Manga & Anime History

漫画・アニメの歴史

From Choju-giga at Kozan-ji to Pokemon

Some of the oldest comic art in Japan is kept in a mountain temple in Kyoto, and it stars a rabbit and a frog.

As a licensed guide, I like to start manga and anime history at a temple, because it shows how long the habit of telling stories in pictures has lasted here. This page follows that thread from medieval scrolls to Pokemon in a few steps. It uses text and links rather than pictures of modern characters, which are protected by copyright, and it points you to official sites and museums instead.

The Scrolls at Kozan-ji鳥獣戯画

The Choju-jinbutsu-giga, usually called Choju-giga, is a set of four ink scrolls in which rabbits, frogs, monkeys and other animals wrestle, swim, race and parody monks. Wikipedia (Choju-jinbutsu-giga) dates the scrolls to the 12th and 13th centuries and notes that the usual attribution to the priest Toba Sojo is uncertain. They belong to Kozan-ji, a temple at Toganoo in the hills north-west of Kyoto (Kozan-ji), and are designated National Treasures.

Dates for the designation differ between sources (1905 in one, 1952 in another), so I will leave the year out. What matters for this story is the style: lively lines, humor, and a sequence that you read as you unroll it. Many people call it the ancestor of manga, which is a fair way to see it, though it is a claim of influence and not a family tree.

Hokusai and the Word Manga北斎漫画

Picture scrolls (emaki) became woodblock-printed books in the Edo period. Katsushika Hokusai's sketchbooks, called Hokusai Manga, began appearing in 1814 and filled many volumes with people, animals, spirits and everyday scenes (Wikipedia (Hokusai Manga)). They were drawing manuals, not comics in the modern sense, but they helped make the word 漫画 familiar. For more on the prints of this era, see the ukiyo-e guide.

Satire, Magazines and Paper Theater風刺漫画と紙芝居

After 1868, Western-style newspapers and humor magazines arrived. Kitazawa Rakuten drew satirical cartoons and is linked with the magazine Tokyo Puck, launched in 1905 (Wikipedia (Kitazawa Rakuten)). In the 1930s, street storytellers used kamishibai, picture cards slid in a small wooden stage, to sell sweets and tell serial stories to children (Wikipedia (Kamishibai)). After the war, cheap rental comics (kashihon) carried on the habit of reading serial stories on paper.

Tezuka, Toei and Astro Boy手塚治虫

Osamu Tezuka's New Treasure Island (1947) is often marked as the start of postwar story manga (Wikipedia (New Treasure Island)). Its movie-like panels and long plots set a pattern. In 1958 Toei released Hakujaden, a color feature based on a Chinese legend (Wikipedia (Hakujaden)). Then on 1 January 1963 Astro Boy began on Japanese television (Wikipedia (Astro Boy, 1963)). Earlier TV animation existed, so I call it the first long-running popular weekly TV anime, not the first of all.

A tip for visitors: Tezuka spent his youth in Takarazuka, Hyogo, and the museum there opened in 1994 (the Takarazuka museum site).

Weekly Shonen Jump and the Boom週刊少年ジャンプ

Weekly Shonen Jump was launched by Shueisha in 1968 (sources give slightly different first-issue dates), and its peak circulation, reported as about 6.5 million copies in the mid-1990s, remains a record for a manga magazine (Wikipedia (Weekly Shonen Jump)). Those years produced Dragon Ball and many others, and manga became a mass medium for readers of every age.

Ghibli, Pokemon and the World世界へ

Studio Ghibli was founded in 1985 (Wikipedia (Studio Ghibli)). Spirited Away won the Golden Bear in Berlin in 2002 and the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature in 2003 (Wikipedia (Spirited Away)). The Pokemon anime began in Japan in 1997 and became a worldwide franchise. More recently, Demon Slayer: Mugen Train (2020) became one of the biggest box-office hits in Japanese history, though the exact totals differ by source, so I will not quote one. If you want to see real places behind these stories, start with the anime pilgrimage guides.

Where to Visit訪ねる

  • Kozan-ji, Kyoto. The temple that keeps Choju-giga. Check Kozan-ji for what is on view and for opening hours.
  • Kyoto International Manga Museum. A large manga archive in a former school building; see the Kyoto International Manga Museum site for hours and closed days, which vary by source.
  • Osamu Tezuka Manga Museum, Takarazuka. Around a 10-minute walk from Takarazuka Station according to the city's tourist listings; the museum is closed on Mondays in several listings, so confirm on the Takarazuka museum site.
  • Ghibli Museum, Mitaka, Tokyo. Entry by advance reservation only (the Ghibli Museum ticket page).
  • Mizuki Shigeru Road, Sakaiminato, Tottori. A street of about 800 meters lined with bronze yokai statues from GeGeGe no Kitaro; the count varies from about 150 to 178 by source (GLTJP, the MLIT multilingual database).

For a guided option in Kyoto, you can browse manga and anime tours on Viator. I have not tried them, so check each page.

Frequently Asked Questionsよくある質問

What is the oldest manga in Japan?
There is no single agreed answer, because it depends on how you define manga. The most common starting point is the Choju-jinbutsu-giga (the "frolicking animals" scrolls) kept at Kozan-ji in Kyoto, in which rabbits, frogs and monkeys act like people. Wikipedia (Choju-jinbutsu-giga) dates them to the 12th and 13th centuries, though some popular sources give a slightly earlier range.
Who invented the word manga?
The word was in use in the Edo period. Hokusai's sketchbooks, titled Hokusai Manga from 1814, helped spread it (Wikipedia (Hokusai Manga)), and cartoonists such as Kitazawa Rakuten used it for satirical comics in the early 1900s (Wikipedia (Kitazawa Rakuten)).
Why is Osamu Tezuka called the god of manga?
He drew the hit New Treasure Island in 1947 (Wikipedia (New Treasure Island)), built a cinematic style of panel layout, and later produced Astro Boy, which began on television on 1 January 1963 (Wikipedia (Astro Boy, 1963)). Many later artists say they grew up on his work.
Was Astro Boy the first anime?
No. Animated films existed in Japan decades earlier, and Toei's Hakujaden (1958) was a feature in color. Astro Boy is usually described as the first long-running, popular weekly TV anime series, which is the safer claim.
Can I see the Choju-giga scrolls in person?
The scrolls are National Treasures and are rarely on public display, and when they are shown it is usually in museum exhibitions. Kozan-ji is a working temple in the mountains north-west of Kyoto, so check its own site for what visitors can see on the day.
Which museum is best for a first visit?
For history, the Kyoto International Manga Museum (the Kyoto International Manga Museum site) is a good start, since it holds a large archive of manga. For one artist, the Osamu Tezuka Manga Museum in Takarazuka (the Takarazuka museum site) is the classic choice. The Ghibli Museum in Mitaka is reservation-only (the Ghibli Museum ticket page), so book ahead.

Final Thoughtsむすびに

From a frog and a rabbit wrestling on a scroll to a worldwide industry, the story is one long line of artists who liked to draw people and animals in motion. If you only have time for one stop, make it Kozan-ji in the quiet hills of Kyoto.
