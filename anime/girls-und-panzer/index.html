Girls und Panzer Pilgrimage: A Seaside Shrine in Oarai

ガールズ＆パンツァー 聖地巡礼

Isosaki Shrine, the torii on the rocks and a whole town that became the set

WRITTEN BY SACRED JAPAN ／ LICENSED NATIONAL TOUR GUIDE (ENGLISH)

Most anime pilgrimages are a single shrine and a nice photo. Oarai is different: the whole seaside town in Ibaraki Prefecture has embraced Girls und Panzer, with life-size character panels along the shopping street and a gallery in the harbor station. What I like, as a shrine guide, is that the real star of the walk is old and quiet: Oarai Isosaki Shrine, founded in 856, with a torii standing on rocks in the Pacific.

💡 Quick Visitor Snapshot

  • The town: Oarai, Ibaraki. From Tokyo, take the JR Joban Line to Mito (about 2 hours), then the Kashima Rinkai Railway to Oarai Station (about 15 minutes, not covered by JR passes).
  • The shrine: Oarai Isosaki Shrine, 6890 Isohamacho. Bus about 10 minutes from the station, or a walk of about 25 minutes.
  • Getting around: rent a bike at Umimachi Terrace beside the station, or use the loop bus.
  • Time needed: a full day for the town plus the shrine.

Oarai Isosaki Shrine

Tradition says the shrine was founded in 856, when two deities descended on the Oarai coast. They are Daikoku-sama, for nation-building and prosperity, and Sukunahikona, for medicine. It was destroyed in the war of 1558 to 1570 and rebuilt in 1690 with patrons including Tokugawa Mitsukuni.

The shrine has three torii in separate places. The Kamiiso Torii stands on rocks on the coast, facing east. On New Year's Day the sun rises between its pillars, according to the Ibaraki tourism guide. The sea itself is the object of worship, so please do not climb the rocks or move stones.

Here is the same torii on a rougher day. The pillars stand on a reef, and the sea washes over the base on every wave. From the shore it looks small and very alone, which is exactly why people come at sunrise. The rocks are part of the sacred site, not a viewing platform, so shoot from the shore.

The shrine itself is up the hill above the town. The walk is short and goes in a clear order: the big white torii, the long stone steps, the guardian gate, the worship hall and, last, the main sanctuary.

The big white torii stands beside the road at the foot of the hill, with a black-and-gold nameplate reading Oarai Isosaki Jinja. On the left it catches low winter light from the roadside. On the right, from the plaza, you look straight up the stone steps, and a second torii is visible at the top. Guides widely link it to the scene where tanks pass under a torii, so expect fans to stop here for photos.

The steps are long and steep, with a steel handrail down the middle, and the climb is the real exercise of the day. Take it slowly, and keep to the side if people are coming down. This is the flight that guides link to the tank scene in the film, so it is a good place to pause and look back at the sea.

At the top you reach the Zuishin-mon, the guardian gate. A pair of komainu in deep brown-red glaze flank it. Notice boards on the gate describe restoration work on the shrine buildings after the 2011 earthquake. The board in this photo lists monthly rites on the 1st and 15th at 10 a.m., and a sign by the right-hand komainu points to the counter for prayers and goshuin. Check the shrine for current times.

Inside the gate the mood changes. A wide lawn, stone lanterns and a few trees surround the worship hall, whose red front frame is hung with a shimenawa rope and paper streamers. Sake barrels from a local brewery are stacked beside the hall. This is the place to make your offering, and the etiquette notes just below apply.

The honden, the main sanctuary, sits behind and above the hall. This photo was taken from outside the enclosure, over a corridor roof. It shows a thick thatched roof, crossed finials (chigi) on the ridge, gold crests and carved brackets under the eaves. Most visitors never see it this closely, so it is worth looking up from the hall.

Ema, Hours and Early Mornings

This is what the ema wall looks like. Fans draw the girls and their tanks, and many plates carry wishes for the town and the series. A few legible plates are dated December 2012 or 2013, and one prays for Oarai's growth and a second season of the show. They are small pieces of fan art, but they are also real prayers, so read them gently and do not touch or move anyone's plate.

Visiting fans draw character illustrations on many of the ema. One guide says a large Girls und Panzer ema is visible only while the Zuishinmon gate is open. Published hours differ by source (one lists 8:30 to 18:00 and says staff are unavailable on Wednesdays), so check the shrine's own site. For the Kamiiso Torii, go early. One guide recommends morning for the best light and the fewest people.

The Town Walk

Start at Umimachi Terrace beside the station for maps and bike rental. One guide counts 54 life-size character panels along Magarimatsu shopping street, and character standees run along the coast route to the shrine. Please treat these as a shared neighborhood. The streets are residential, so do not touch the panels, and the shops and inns are working businesses.

The Anko Festival

The Oarai Anko Festival, a monkfish festival, is held in November. A 2019 report recorded a record 140,000 visitors at the 23rd edition, with monkfish soup, Girls und Panzer character cutouts and a talk show by cast members. Dates and details change each year, so confirm them before you plan your trip around it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is Girls und Panzer set in real life?
Oarai, a seaside town in Ibaraki Prefecture. The Oarai Isosaki Shrine, its torii and the shopping streets all appear in the story or film.
How do I get to Oarai from Tokyo?
Take the JR Joban Line to Mito (about 2 hours), then the Kashima Rinkai Railway to Oarai Station (about 15 minutes). The second line is a private railway, so JR passes do not cover it.
When is the Oarai Anko Festival?
It is held in November. Check the Oarai tourism site for the exact dates each year.
Can I climb on the rocks under the torii?
No. The offshore rocks are off-limits. Do not climb them or move stones.
⛩️

Oarai is a good example of a town that said yes to fans and kept its shrine and sea at the center. Come for the tanks, and stay for the sunrise at the torii.
